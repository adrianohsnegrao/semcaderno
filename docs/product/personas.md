# Personas

These personas are initial assumptions for product planning. They must be validated through user interviews, observation, or pilot usage before being treated as confirmed market facts.

## Persona 1: Owner-Operator

The owner works at the counter, sells directly to customers, buys supplies, and keeps records in a notebook. The business may be a bar, small grocery store, snack shop, or neighborhood convenience store.

Needs:

- Record sales quickly during busy periods.
- Know who bought on credit and still owes money.
- Avoid losing payment history.
- Understand whether the month is going well in plain language.

Risks:

- May reject the product if it feels like an ERP.
- May not trust reports if they cannot connect them to visible sales and payments.
- May use the system from a phone or shared device.

## Persona 2: Family Helper or Trusted Employee

This user helps operate the business but may not own it. They may record sales, take product photos, or send collection messages.

Needs:

- Simple screens with limited room for mistakes.
- Permissions appropriate to their role.
- Clear confirmation when recording sales or payments.

Risks:

- Excess permissions may expose sensitive business information.
- Missing audit history may make it hard to understand who changed financial records.

## Persona 3: Merchant Who Sells on Credit

This merchant commonly allows known customers to buy now and pay later. The notebook is used mainly to track "fiado".

Needs:

- See "Quem está devendo".
- Record partial payments.
- Review customer purchase and payment history.
- Send polite payment reminders with Pix information.

Risks:

- Balance-only tracking can hide mistakes.
- Deleting or editing history silently can create disputes with customers.

## Accessibility Considerations

Across personas, the product should assume:

- Users may have low digital confidence.
- Users may operate under time pressure.
- Devices may be small or old.
- Text must be readable and plain.
- Important actions should be hard to trigger accidentally.

## Open Validation Questions

- Who is allowed to record payments in the real business routine?
- Does the owner need separate visibility from employees in the first release?
- Are product photos important for recognition, customer communication, or both?
- How often do businesses need to correct a sale or payment after recording it?
